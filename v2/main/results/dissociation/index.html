Result

We next tested chemical bond breaking, which forces a smooth transition from a single dominant electronic configuration around equilibrium geometry to a multiconfigurational structure at dissociation, which has been a long-standing stress test for computational methods. For simple hydrogen chains, in which two to eight hydrogen atoms are pulled apart simultaneously, our method's dissociation curves closely track near-exact reference calculations across all chain lengths tested.

Extending to real multi-bond chemistry, our method also correctly separates ethylene's carbon-carbon double bond, nitrogen's triple bond, and acetylene's triple bond into their correct fragments [Kuchitsu 1998] [Huber and Herzberg 1979], capturing subtleties such as an avoided crossing between two dissociation pathways in acetylene that a less complete treatment could miss [Siegbahn 1981].

The most demanding case is the carbon dimer, C2, whose quadruple bond has remained a long-standing challenge in electronic structure theory [Shaik et al. 2013]. Our calculations reproduce near-exact benchmark energies [Holmes et al. 2017] and match the experimentally measured bond length and binding energy [Martin 1992] [Huber and Herzberg 1979]. Across all of these cases, spanning one to four breaking bonds, the method requires no case-by-case adjustment.

PECs for H4, H6 and H8 from SSR(n,n)/BH&HLYP compared with FCI. H4 uses the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set for both SSR and FCI; H6 and H8 use 6-31G*, a smaller basis set required because FCI becomes computationally intractable for larger ones. Triangles denote single-point FCI references computed with larger basis sets: aug-cc-pVDZ (H6) and cc-pVDZ (H8). Since FCI with a small basis set cannot fully capture the correlation energy, the FCI curves of H6 and H8 lie somewhat above the corresponding SSR curves. DFT-based methods are less sensitive to basis set size than FCI [Goerigk and Grimme 2011] [Bursch et al. 2022], so the depth of the SSR minima at equilibrium geometries agrees better with the FCI single-point values obtained at the larger basis sets. Setup and reference

Definitions

Re, Emin: minimum of the quartic through the 5 grid points around the lowest one; B1Δg of C2: the lowest grid point.

Te = Emin(state) − Emin(S0)

De(C2) = 2 E(C 3P) − Emin(S0)

E(C 3P): SSR(4,4)/M08-SO/cc-pV5Z, 2 E(C) = −75.6661374 Eh [Komarov et al. 2026, SI].

Separated fragments n E(H): the high-spin ROKS chain at Rmax; molecules: fragments at each segment; methods in the setup line.

ni: natural occupations of the SSR state (eigenvalues of its active one-particle density, unrelaxed; Manual, natural orbitals), summing to the number of active electrons. FON: fractional occupation of an SA-REKS orbital; ni above is a property of the SSR state. Energies in Eh, Te and De in eV, R in Å.
