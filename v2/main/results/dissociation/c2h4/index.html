C2H4: SSR(4,4) dissociation

The ground-state PEC smoothly correlates the equilibrium geometry with the asymptotic energy of two isolated 3B1 methylene fragments, confirming accurate ground-state double-bond dissociation; near equilibrium, SSR(4,4) matches KS DFT, but KS DFT fails qualitatively at extended bond lengths, diverging unphysically upward.

SSR(4,4) potential energy curves (PECs) of singlet states of C2H4 under C=C bond stretch with fixed experimental geometry [Craig et al. 2006]. Symmetries: 1Ag (blue), 1B1u (red), and 1B2g (green) for the named states; KS DFT (dashed grey). Short dashed lines show asymptotic energies of two CH2 fragments (3B1, 1A1, 1B1). Gray arrow: vertical B1B1u ← X1Ag transition compared with the theoretical best estimate (TBE) [Véril et al. 2021].

At equilibrium, SSR(4,4) predicts the vertical excitation energy to B1B1u at 7.85 eV, close to the TBE value of 7.93 eV [Véril et al. 2021]. This state dissociates into two 1B1 methylenes; since two such states form two distinct linear combinations, a second excited state (D1Ag) converges to the same asymptote, splitting by only 0.14 eV at 4.0 Å from in-phase/out-of-phase coupling. The A1B2g and C1Ag states converge to the (1A1)CH2 + (1A1)CH2 limit; near equilibrium, A1B2g shows initial Rydberg character before an avoided crossing with a valence state of the same symmetry. All five singlet-state asymptotic energies map consistently onto the isolated-fragment energies, with minor discrepancies from differing state-averaging conditions. Overall, SSR(4,4) gives a quantitatively reliable, balanced description of multiple-bond dissociation across the full surface.

Calculations

For ethylene, SA-REKS(4,4) orbital optimization utilized a four-state ensemble comprising the PPS1, OSS1, and OSS2 singlet configurations alongside a high-spin (HS) quintet electronic configuration. Incorporating the high-spin quintet state accelerates SCF convergence and minimizes the total iteration count. The subsequent SI step included 18 configurations to comprehensively span all physical configurations arising from the two coupling schemes within the (4,4) active space. For the dissociation fragments, singlet state energies of CH2 were computed using SSR(2,2) calculations restricted to the PPS1 and OSS1 configurations, whereas the ground triplet state was modelled via the spin-restricted open-shell Kohn-Sham (ROKS) method. All electronic structure calculations were performed using the BH&HLYP density functional paired with the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set. The initial guess orbitals were obtained in a standard KS DFT calculation with the same density functional and basis set. The potential energy curves were scanned at an increment of 0.1 Å.
