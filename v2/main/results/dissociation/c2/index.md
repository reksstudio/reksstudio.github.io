# C<sub>2</sub>: SSR(6,6) dissociation

SSR(6,6) accurately maps the two lowest states (X<sup>1</sup>Σ<sub>g</sub><sup>+</sup> and a<sup>3</sup>Π<sub>u</sub>), aligning closely with the reference points. The calculated singlet-triplet splitting (T<sub>e</sub>) is exceptionally narrow at 0.03 eV, close to the experimental 0.09 eV \[[Martin 1992](https://reksstudio.github.io/v2/manual/reference/index.md#martin1992-c2)]. The A<sup>1</sup>Π<sub>u</sub> excited state's structure and energetics also agree reasonably with experiment. SSR underestimates the B<sup>1</sup>Δ<sub>g</sub> T<sub>e</sub> by ∼0.49 eV, though its PEC minimum matches the experimental R<sub>e</sub> well.

This ability to capture quadruple-bond dissociation reflects the localized nature of SSR's correlation treatment: non-dynamic correlation within only three – not four – electron pairs suffices to describe C<sub>2</sub> bond cleavage. Near equilibrium, C–C stabilization arises mainly from configuration mixing via 2σ<sub>u</sub> → 3σ<sub>g</sub> promotion, seen as fractional occupations in the two σ orbitals, while the π orbitals remain largely doubly occupied (1π<sub>u</sub>) or empty (1π<sub>g</sub>).

As the bond elongates, the σ occupations converge to integer values of two (2σ<sub>u</sub>) and zero (3σ<sub>g</sub>), neutralizing σ-bonding, while the π manifold redistributes to single occupancy of bonding and anti-bonding orbitals – homolytic cleavage of the two π bonds. Thus, non-dynamic correlation within three electron pairs (one σ, two π) suffices to model the quadruple-bond dissociation, consistent with chemical intuition.

SSR(6,6) accurately reproduces the ground- and excited-state character of this notoriously difficult molecule, yielding a dissociation energy (D<sub>e</sub>) of 6.29 eV, in excellent agreement with the experimental 6.32±0.2 eV \[[Huber and Herzberg 1979](https://reksstudio.github.io/v2/manual/reference/index.md#huber1979-diatomic)].

## Calculations

The SA-REKS(6,6) orbital optimizations for C<sub>2</sub> were performed using a nine-state ensemble. In addition to the five configurations shared with N<sub>2</sub> and C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>2</sub>, this ensemble included four triplet configurations generated by one-electron spin-flip transitions from the 1π<sub>u</sub> to 3σ<sub>g</sub> orbitals. These transitions arise across all orbital coupling schemes compatible with <sup>3</sup>Π<sub>u</sub> total symmetry. At the subsequent state-interaction stage, complete sets of singlet and triplet configurations were utilized. To determine the dissociation energy, the energies of isolated carbon atoms were calculated via the SSR(4,4) method. This was achieved by assigning one 2s and two 2p orbitals to the active space, followed by an orbital optimization over an ensemble of PPS1, OSS1, OSS2, and HS states. The resulting energies for the <sup>3</sup>P triplet state of carbon – comprising two degenerate components with m<sub>S</sub> = 0 and m<sub>L</sub> = ±1 at −37.8330687 Hartree – differ from the ROKS triplet state energy (−37.8300017 Hartree) by roughly 0.08 eV. All calculations were carried out using the M08-SO density functional paired with the cc-pV5Z basis set.
