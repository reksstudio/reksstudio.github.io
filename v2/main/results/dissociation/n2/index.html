N2: SSR(6,6) dissociation

Using SSR(6,6)/BH&HLYP, the N2 ground-state PEC evolves smoothly from equilibrium (calc.: 1.096 Å; exp.: 1.0977 Å [Huber and Herzberg 1979]) to two isolated ground-state (4S) nitrogen atoms. We did not classify excited states by symmetry or rigorously benchmark vertical excitation energies, since these depend on functional choice; nevertheless, SSR(6,6)/BH&HLYP reasonably reproduces the lowest vertical excitation and captures triple-bond dissociation with conceptual fidelity, free of unphysical artifacts or symmetry-breaking discontinuities.

Calculations

The SSR(6,6) calculations for N2 and C2H2 were performed using a five-state ensemble – comprising PPS1, OSS1, OSS2, OSS3, and HS – for orbital optimization, combined with a complete set of configurations for the state-interaction stage. Dissociation fragments were computed via the ROKS high-spin method. All simulations utilized the BH&HLYP density functional alongside the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set.
