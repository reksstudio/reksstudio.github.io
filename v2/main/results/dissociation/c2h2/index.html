C2H2: SSR(6,6) dissociation

Acetylene (C2H2) shows analogous behavior. The pathway correlating with triple C≡C character dissociates to two CH radicals in their 4Σ− states [Siegbahn 1981]. Since isolated CH's ground state is 2Π, an avoided crossing occurs between pathways converging to the (4Σ−)CH + (4Σ−)CH and (2Π)CH + (2Π)CH asymptotes [Siegbahn 1981]. SSR(6,6) captures this crossing accurately, with the 1Σg+ PECs converging smoothly to their limits after traversing the crossing region. Together, these results show REKS/SSR delivers a chemically sound description of multiple-bond cleavage while resolving fine topological features of complex dissociation pathways.

Calculations

The SSR(6,6) calculations for N2 and C2H2 were performed using a five-state ensemble – comprising PPS1, OSS1, OSS2, OSS3, and HS – for orbital optimization, combined with a complete set of configurations for the state-interaction stage. Dissociation fragments were computed via the ROKS high-spin method. All simulations utilized the BH&HLYP density functional alongside the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set.
