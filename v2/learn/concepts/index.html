Concepts

Active space, core, virtual

REKS [N, M] places N active electrons in M active orbitals with fractional occupation. The other occupied orbitals form a doubly occupied core shared by every determinant; the orbitals above the active space are virtual.

Used in: P1. One pair.

Unit

The active space is a list of units, one token each (REKS_GEMINAL_WIDTHS); K is the width, the number of orbitals of the unit. An ordinary pair is the unit 2: an electron geminal of width 2, two electrons in a bonding and an antibonding orbital, na+nb=2 (Geminals).

Width ≥ 3, holes, doublets

Unit Electron Hole
Geminal K, K ≥ 2: 2 electrons; FON n, Σ n = 2 "hK", K ≥ 3: 2K − 2 electrons; FON h = 2 − n, Σ h = 2
Doublet "dK", K ≥ 1: 1 electron; no FON "hdK", K ≥ 2: 2K − 1 electrons; no FON

A hole unit is the particle–hole mirror of the electron unit: a width-2 hole geminal would be the ordinary pair and a width-1 hole doublet the SOMO, hence K ≥ 3 and K ≥ 2. An active space holds at least one geminal and at most one doublet; for odd N without a doublet token the SOMO is the implicit d1.

The PPS geminal over the slots p1,…,pK of one electron geminal of width K:

Φ0=∑k=1Ksknpk2|pk𝑝̄k|,∑k=1Knpk=2,s=(+,−,…,−).

For K=2 this is Φ0 of Geminals. The anchor slot carries the + sign: the first slot of an electron geminal, default signs (+,−,…,−); the last slot of a hole geminal, default signs (−,…,−,+). For K≥3 the signs of the other K−1 slots are variational: the FON solver compares the 2K−1 sign patterns, starting from the default, and keeps the lowest-energy one; the result is published as REKS ROOT SIGNS (REKS M_ROOT SIGNS, … for higher generations). A doublet carries no FON; its aufbau slot is the first orbital of an electron doublet, the last of a hole doublet.

With HF and δ→∞ (reks_fon_interp_delta 1010), f(x)=x (Coupling Δ and factor f) and E(PPS1) equals ⟨Ψ|H|Ψ⟩ of the product Ψ of the geminals Φ0 in the converged orbitals, H the Hamiltonian of the active space with the core frozen. A product of geminals is GVB, not CAS: it has no correlation between the geminals, so CASCI in the same orbitals is lower for two geminals; for one geminal CASCI equals E(PPS1). Slots and seeds of each unit kind: Slots of each unit kind.

Used in: P1. One pair, Lone pair over three orbitals: BH, REKS(2,3) 3, Hole geminal: REKS(4,3) h3, Electron doublet over two orbitals: REKS(3,4) 2 d2, Implicit SOMO: REKS(3,3) 2 d1.

Ensemble Kohn–Sham reference and FONs

The non-interacting reference density, with fractional occupation numbers (FONs) nq for the orbitals pseudo-degenerate at the Fermi level, is

ρs=∑k∈core2|φk|2+∑q∈activenq|φq|2,
0≤nq≤2,∑qnq=N.

The energy functional follows from integration over the electron–electron coupling strength λ from 0 to 1 at λ-independent FONs. The orbitals and the FONs are optimized variationally (Filatov, WIREs Comput. Mol. Sci. 2015, 5, 146).

Used in: P1. One pair.

Microstate

A microstate L is a Slater determinant of the shared orbitals with fixed integer spin-orbital occupations nq,Lσ. Its energy is an ordinary Kohn–Sham energy with the chosen functional (local, non-local, hybrid or range-separated):

EL=E[ρLα,ρLβ].

Used in: P2. Broken π bond.

Geminals

An active orbital pair (p,q) holds two electrons; its FONs satisfy

np+nq=2.

There are six simplest geminals, three singlets and three triplets with m=0,±1:

Φ0=12(np|p𝑝̄|−nq|q𝑞̄|)perfectly pairedΦ2=12(nq|p𝑝̄|+np|q𝑞̄|)doubly excitedΦ1=12(|p𝑞̄|+|q𝑝̄|)open-shell singletΦT0=12(|p𝑞̄|−|q𝑝̄|)triplet, m=0ΦT+=|pq|,ΦT−=|𝑝̄𝑞̄|triplet, m=±1.

A configuration of REKS(M,M) is a product of these four m=0 geminals; ΦT+ and ΦT− enter only the spin-coupled configurations of 2S≥4 (Spin manifold and run sector).

Used in: P1. One pair.

Pairing scheme

A pairing scheme partitions the M active orbitals into P=N/2 geminals. With pairs only (REKS(M,M)) each pair joins one orbital of the lower half with one of the upper half:

P=M/2.

With REKS_GEMINAL_WIDTHS unit i spans Ki orbitals, ∑iKi=M: an electron geminal holds 2 electrons, a hole geminal 2Ki−2, a doublet unit 1 or 2Ki−1. Pairing schemes prints all orbitals of each unit.

Scheme 0 pairs the active slots (k=0,1,…, labelled a, b, …) from the outside in: (2,2) (a,b); (4,4) (a,d) & (b,c); (6,6) (a,f) & (b,e) & (c,d):

(k,M−1−k),k=0,…,P−1.

REKS(M,M) with pairs has P! schemes, numbered from 0: one for (2,2), two for (4,4), six for (6,6) (Active space and catalog).

Used in: P4. Two pairs.

Configuration and its energy

A configuration is the antisymmetrized product of the core and one geminal per electron pair within a given scheme:

Ψ=Aˆ[(core)Φt1(p1,q1)⋯ΦtP(pP,qP)].

One scheme carries 3P singlet configurations, named by type (Active space and catalog). Its energy is a weighted sum of microstate energies, the coefficients being functions of the FONs (Filatov et al., Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 2016, 18, 21040):

EX=∑LCLX({n})EL,∑LCLX=SXX,

the FON normalizes the configuration to 1 for pairs, and possibly below 1 for a configuration with wider geminals (SI Hamiltonian and overlap).

Used in: P2. Broken π bond.

Coupling Δ and factor f

The energy expressions contain, for each pair (a,b), the coupling Δab in place of the exchange integral Kab, multiplied by f(x) in place of the FON radical x:

Δab=12[E(a𝑏̄)+E(b𝑎̄)−E(ab)−E(𝑎̄𝑏̄)],
f(x)=x1−x+δ2(1+δ),x=nanb.

For REKS(2,2), with f=f(nanb), the configuration energies are

EPPS=na2E(a𝑎̄)+nb2E(b𝑏̄)−fΔabEDES=nb2E(a𝑎̄)+na2E(b𝑏̄)+fΔabEOSS=E(a𝑏̄)+E(b𝑎̄)−12[E(ab)+E(𝑎̄𝑏̄)]ET=12[E(ab)+E(𝑎̄𝑏̄)]

EOSS and ET carry no FON.

As the closed-shell limit (na→2) is approached, the factor f suppresses the Δ term, thereby avoiding the double-counting of correlation by both the formalism and the functional. At na=nb=1 it equals 1 (Moreira et al., J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2007, 3, 764). δ is REKS_FON_INTERP_DELTA (default 0.4, Fractional occupation numbers (FONs)).

Used in: P2. Broken π bond, Lone pair over three orbitals: BH, REKS(2,3) 3.

FON set and generation

The generation of a configuration is its number of open pairs: Φ1, and a triplet of geminals (ΦT+, ΦT0, ΦT−) in a manifold of positive 2S. A FON set keeps the FONs of one generation, pairing scheme, and run sector; all configurations of that set use the same FONs. Φ0 and Φ2 of one pair use the same FONs; Φ1 and ΦT0 use none. FON-set labels: FON sets.

Used in: P4. Two pairs.

SA-REKS ensemble and weights

The SCF minimizes the ensemble energy over the orbitals and the FONs read by the SA configurations, at fixed weights wX (ensemble variational principle: Gross et al., Phys. Rev. A 1988, 37, 2805; SA-REKS: Filatov, Top. Curr. Chem. 2016, 368, 97):

ESA=∑XwXEX=∑LCLEL,CL=∑XwXCLX,
0≤wX≤1,∑XwX=1.

The default weights are equal (SA_REKS_WEIGHTS). ESA is a weighted average of configuration energies, not the energy of a state; the variational principle bounds ESA, not an individual EX. Extra determinants can be added to the ensemble using SA_REKS_EXTRA.

Used in: P2. Broken π bond, A triplet configuration in the SA ensemble.

Orbitals, orbital energies, SCF convergence

The orbitals satisfy, with orbital-specific Fock operators Fˆq,

fqFˆqφq=∑pφpεpq,fq=nq/2.

At convergence, the Lagrangian εpq is symmetric (generalized Brillouin condition) but not diagonal, so the orbitals are not canonical. The printed orbital energies are the diagonal of the SA-REKS coupling Fock matrix in the MO basis and carry no Koopmans meaning; ionization energies come from EKT (REKS_EKT). The SCF is converged when ESA is stationary and the orbital gradient vanishes (SCF convergence engines).

Used in: P3. Seed orbitals.

Spin-polarized configurations

In REKS(4,4) the double open-shell singlet (DOSS) and the doubly spin-polarized state (DSPS) both arise from two simultaneous OSS-type one-electron transitions between the active orbitals: DOSS couples two open-shell singlet pairs to an overall singlet, DSPS can be viewed as two triplet excitations coupled to an overall singlet, and the two are orthogonalized (Filatov et al., J. Chem. Phys. 2017, 147, 064104). Fully open configurations of different schemes overlap (overlap −½ for the two DOSS configurations of REKS(4,4)); the catalog replaces them by a set orthogonalized by sequential Gram–Schmidt procedure across schemes, the spin-polarized configurations (SPS) DOSS_SPS_k, T_OSS_SPS_k, …, in which each member k after the first is orthogonal to the preceding members (Komarov et al., ChemRxiv 2026). They carry no FON.

Used in: P4. Two pairs.

Spin manifold and run sector

A manifold is one total spin 2S of the catalog. The sectors of a run are the manifolds addressed by any SA block or SI group, in ascending 2S, sector 0 being the lowest. Every configuration has the canonical total MS (0 for even N, 1/2 for odd N), so all sectors share one determinant space and one set of orbitals, and the molecular multiplicity remains canonical (1 for even N, 2 for odd N) (Active space, Spin manifolds (2S) and multi-manifold runs).

A 2S = 2 configuration holds one ΦT0. In 2S ≥ 4 (TT_SPS_0, TTT_SPS_0) the nT triplet geminals couple to S=nT through ΦT+, ΦT0, ΦT− with Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, e.g.

TT_SPS_0=16ΦT−ΦT++23ΦT0ΦT0+16ΦT+ΦT−.

Used in: P5. Spin states.

SI cassette

A cassette is a set of configurations of one manifold over which the SI Hamiltonian and overlap are built and diagonalized after the SCF, in the converged SA orbitals. It is chosen independently of the SA pool. A run may hold several cassettes, each its own eigenproblem (SI eigensolver). The label kSI-lSA-REKS(N,M) denotes k SI configurations in the orbitals of an SA ensemble of l configurations (SI_REKS_CONFIGS).

Used in: P2. Broken π bond, P4. Two pairs.

SI-only FONs

FONs that no SA configuration uses (higher generations, or schemes absent from the SA pool) are optimized once after the SCF, with the SA FONs held fixed. Each SI-only geminal belongs to its primary configuration Y, the lowest-index configuration of the cassette among those that use it. Its FONs minimize, at the microstate energies EL of the converged SA orbitals,

EY=∑LCLY({n})EL,η≤np≤2−η,

for a pair; in a geminal of width K each free FON is ≥η and their sum ≤2−η; η = SI_REKS_FON_BOUND_MARGIN (default 10−8). A FON pushed past its bound stops on it and counts as converged there. REKS_<L>_FON does not apply. The geminals of one generation with the same Y are solved jointly; Gauss–Seidel sweeps over these groups repeat until no FON changes by more than 10−7. Output: FONs of the SI-only geminals.

Used in: P4. Two pairs.

SI Hamiltonian and overlap

The diagonal elements of 𝐇 are the configuration energies EX. An off-diagonal element is the sum of the Slater–Condon elements between the microstates of the two configurations, times the FON factors of their geminals; its terms carry microstate energies EL, active Fock elements of the microstates, and active two-electron integrals (pq|rs), the latter unscaled with any functional (table Off-diagonal channel, REKS_REPORT_LEVEL ≥ 3). A one-electron excitation p→q in spin σ from microstate L to L′ takes the bra/ket mean of the two microstate Fock matrices, with the sign of the excitation:

⟨L|Hˆ|L′⟩=±12[FLσ+FL′σ]pq,
FLσ(pq)=hpq+∑c∈core[2(pq|cc)−(pc|cq)]+∑r∈L[(pq|rr)−δσrσ(pr|rq)],

shown for HF; using a DFT functional, the exchange terms carry the exact-exchange fraction (the long-range fraction for the long-range part of a range-separated functional), and the spin-polarized XC potential of the microstate density (core included) is added. A spin-difference term takes the same mean of ±(Fα−Fβ) over two microstates. 𝐒 depends on the FONs only. Between configurations of pairs, it is diagonal within one scheme and non-diagonal across schemes; configurations of a wider geminal overlap within their scheme. Each cassette solves

𝐇𝐜=E𝐒𝐜.

Over (PPS, OSS, DES) the SSR(2,2) Hamiltonian, with 𝐒=𝟏, is

𝐇=(EPPSnaXa−nbXb0naXa−nbXbEOSSnbXa+naXb0nbXa+naXbEDES),
Xa=12[Fa𝑎̄+Fa𝑏̄β]ab,Xb=12[Fb𝑏̄+Fa𝑏̄α]ab.

The catalog holds no PPS–DES coupling; that element is zero. With HF, Xa=Fa𝑎̄(ab) and Xb=Fb𝑏̄(ab). The sign of the OSS row and column follows the orbital phases; E and the states do not depend on it.

Eigenvectors of 𝐒 whose eigenvalue s satisfies

s≤csmax⁡

(smax⁡ the largest eigenvalue of 𝐒, c = SI_REKS_OVERLAP_CUTOFF) are null modes, removed before diagonalization; the rank of 𝐒 depends on the FONs. In full singlet cassettes the 18 configurations of REKS(4,4) span 16 dimensions and the 161 of REKS(6,6) span 115 (SI Hamiltonian and overlap).

Used in: P4. Two pairs.

SI eigensolver

In the basis left after the null modes (dimension r), each cassette solves the standard eigenproblem 𝐀𝐱=θ𝐱 with one of two solvers (SI_REKS_SOLVER); n is the number of configurations of the cassette:

Solver Roots Memory
DIRECT every root, LAPACK on the dense matrix n2
DAVIDSON the lowest k roots, block Davidson on the sparse 𝐇 no dense 𝐇; 𝒪((m+k)r) for the basis, its products and the work vectors, plus dense matrices of order p (m, p below); several r×r arrays when the whole space is solved
AUTO (default) DIRECT for n≤4000, DAVIDSON above

The catalogs shipped with Psi4 end at REKS(6,6) (161 singlet configurations), so AUTO runs DIRECT on them; a cassette above 4000 configurations, from a catalog in PSI4_REKS_CATALOG (Active space and catalog), runs DAVIDSON. Davidson computes

k=min⁡(r, nreq+max⁡(4,⌈nreq/4⌉)),nreq=min⁡(r, max⁡(1, nstore, nprint, 1+rmax⁡)),

nstore, nprint the counts of SI_REKS_STORE_STATES and SI_REKS_PRINT_STATES, rmax⁡ the highest root named in SI_REKS_GRAD, SI_REKS_NAC or SI_REKS_PROPERTY_FOR; a count of -1 stands for the 50 lowest roots. The search space holds up to

m=min⁡(r, max⁡(40, 16k))

vectors; when m=r, the whole space is solved and every root returned. Otherwise, with 𝐀=𝚲+𝐗′𝖳𝐇×𝐗′ (𝚲 the eigenvalues of the diagonal blocks of 𝐀, one per block of 𝐒; 𝐇× the couplings between blocks), the p=min⁡(r,max⁡(8k,1000)) columns of lowest Λ form the P space:

  • start: the k lowest eigenvectors of 𝐀PP (more to complete a cluster within REKS_GRAD_DEGEN_TOL) and one random vector;
  • preconditioner: (θ−𝐀PP)−1 on P, (θ−Λq)−1 outside it;
  • correction: up to two GMRES steps of the Jacobi–Davidson equation on an operator that keeps at most 2 % of the cross-block couplings (the largest |Hij|), its P block equal to 𝐀PP; without kept couplings, the preconditioned residual;
  • collapse: about max⁡(k,⌊2m/5⌋) Ritz vectors plus the previous Ritz vectors of the unconverged roots.

Residuals, θ and the final check use the exact 𝐀. Each root converges to

‖(𝐀−θ)𝐱‖2≤τ,τ=𝚂𝙸_𝚁𝙴𝙺𝚂_𝙳𝙰𝚅𝙸𝙳𝚂𝙾𝙽_𝚁_𝙲𝙾𝙽𝚅𝙴𝚁𝙶𝙴𝙽𝙲𝙴=10−12 Eh;

a root whose residual stalls at the rounding level of its product is accepted there, and that level is printed. When the last computed root lies within REKS_GRAD_DEGEN_TOL of the last requested one, k grows by the margin and the iteration continues. Roots above k are not computed: SSR ENERGIES K=e holds the k roots (r when the whole space is solved). Hexatriene REKS(6,6), singlet and triplet cassettes (r = 115, 117): with the default counts m=r; with 3 stored and printed states k=7, m=112, p=r, and one iteration converges. Output: SI Davidson solver.

Used in: Lowest roots of a large cassette: Davidson.

Coupling damping below 100 % exact exchange

With a functional whose global (short-range) exact-exchange fraction is below 100 %, range-separated functionals included, the off-diagonal elements Hij that the catalog marks as carried only by four-orbital active integrals (pq|rs) are scaled, energies in Eh (Filatov et al., J. Chem. Phys. 2017, 147, 064104, Eq. B50; parameters from Grimme and Waletzke, J. Chem. Phys. 1999, 111, 5645):

Hij→0.619exp⁡[−3.27(Hii−Hjj)4]Hij.

At 100 % exact exchange, no element is scaled. When an element is scaled, the References block (REKS_REPORT_LEVEL ≥ 3) lists Coupling damping (DFT). The exact-exchange fraction is in the XC evaluation line of the setup block (Run report).

Adiabatic (SSR) states

An adiabatic state is an eigenvector 𝐜k of one cassette:

𝐇𝐜k=Ek𝐒𝐜k.

Roots are 0-based and sorted by energy; they are labeled by the spin letter (S, D, T, Qa, Q, Sx, Sp, Oc, N, De, U, Du, Tr for 2S = 0 … 12), counted from 0 on the lowest manifold (S0, D0) and from 1 on every other (T1, Q1) (Spin manifolds (2S) and multi-manifold runs). Physical roots exclude the overlap null modes and ghost roots more than 100 Eh above the lowest root (Ghost states and a collapsed SI spectrum); SSR ENERGIES K=e holds every physical root of cassette e, on the Davidson path its computed roots (SI eigensolver, SSR state energies).

Used in: P2. Broken π bond, P5. Spin states.

Chirgwin–Coulson weights

The weight of configuration j in a state is

ωj=cj(𝐒𝐜)j,∑jωj=1.

In the non-orthogonal configuration set, an individual ωj can be negative. Adiabatic states prints them in % (column w_j).

Used in: P4. Two pairs.

Unrelaxed and relaxed densities

The unrelaxed density of states I, J is the one-particle (transition) density of the model wavefunctions at the fixed orbitals and FONs:

⟨I|Eˆpq|J⟩.

Every SI_REKS_ANALYSIS property uses it (Properties). The relaxed density Pr of a state adds the orbital- and FON-response terms of the coupled-perturbed REKS (CP-REKS, Z-vector) solve (Filatov et al., J. Chem. Phys. 2017, 147, 034113), so that its dipole is the energy derivative

μ=−dEIdF.

It is formed for the SI_REKS_PROPERTY_FOR states. Pr is not N-representable: it can have negative natural occupations, and bond indices from it are stored but not printed (Bond indices of the relaxed density).

Used in: P6. Forces, Dipoles, charges, NTOs, relaxed properties and EKT.
